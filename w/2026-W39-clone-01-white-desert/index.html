Camps and flights on the polar plateau

ANTARCTICA

The Last Continent

Six hours from Cape Town. A runway of blue ice. No port, no road, no town within two thousand kilometres.

Our Season

Between November and February, during the short polar summer, the sun does not set. Flights run from Cape Town on a weather window that opens and closes without notice.

33° 58′ 17″ S · 18° 36′ 13″ E

Cape Town

One night before the ice. Kit check in the afternoon, weather briefing at six, wheels up before dawn.

5 hrs 10 min · 4 200 km

To the end of the earth

A private jet crosses the Southern Ocean. No land below for four hours. The cabin stays warm; outside it is minus fifty.

71° 32′ S · 8° 49′ E

Wolf Fang runway

Blue ice, swept by wind until it is hard enough to land on. No tarmac was ever laid here.

Six pods · two guides

Camp

Heated pods, running water, and a guide for every two guests. The sun does not set between November and February.

Ridgeway

Our original camp, newly rebuilt. Exposed rock and freshwater lakes — a side of Antarctica seldom seen.

[ 70° 48′ 00″ S, 11° 23′ 00″ E ]

Relay Base

Inspired by astronauts, used by explorers. As close as you can get to leaving Earth without stepping off the planet.

[ 71° 32′ 47″ S, 8° 50′ 11″ E ]

Field Camp

Six pods on the plateau, moved each season as the ice shifts. Nothing is left behind when it goes.

[ 71° 57′ 12″ S, 8° 22′ 40″ E ]

How it works

  1. Consultation

    An adventure to Antarctica starts with a call. We map the dates, the camp and the flights before anything is booked.

  2. Planning

    Medical forms, kit list, insurance. We send the list and check it twice before departure.

  3. Cape Town

    One night in Cape Town, a kit check, and the weather briefing for the ice runway.

  4. The flight

    Five hours ten minutes on a private jet to a runway of blue ice at 71°S.

  5. On the ice

    Heated pods, running water, and a guide for every two guests.
