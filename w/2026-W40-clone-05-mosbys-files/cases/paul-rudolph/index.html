Paul Rudolph
Born:
October 23, 1918 — Elkton, Kentucky, US
Died:
August 8, 1997 — New York City, US (age 78)

Rudolph began in Sarasota, Florida, with light houses of wood, glass and adjustable screens built for a hot climate. By the late 1950s he had moved to heavy concrete and complex interior sections.

As chair of the architecture school at Yale from 1958 to 1965 he built the school its own building: dozens of levels on a few floors, ribbed bush-hammered concrete and rooms that open into each other vertically.

He drew in dense one-point perspective sections. Later in his career many of his commissions came from Southeast Asia.

Yale Art and Architecture Building (1963)

New Haven. Ribbed concrete, split-level studios and a tall reading room. Restored and renamed Rudolph Hall in 2008.

Orange County Government Center (1971)

Goshen, New York. Stacked concrete trays and cantilevers around small courts. Partly demolished and rebuilt in the 2010s.

Boston Government Service Center (1971)

A curved concrete complex around a plaza, with a sculpted stair tower. Only part of the original plan was ever built.

Burroughs Wellcome Headquarters (1972)

Research Triangle Park, North Carolina. Angled concrete frames stepping along a wooded ridge. Demolished in 2021.
