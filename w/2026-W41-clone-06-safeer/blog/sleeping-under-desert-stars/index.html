Sleeping Under Desert Stars

(Details)

Introduction

A night in a desert camp is the part of a trip most people remember. The air is dry, the sky is dark, and the only sound is the wind on the tent. A little planning makes that night comfortable.

(Highlights)

Things to Try

  • Pick a Sheltered Site
  • Set the tent behind a dune or a rock wall. Wind picks up after sunset and drops again before dawn.
  • Pack for Cold Nights
  • Desert temperatures can fall by 20 degrees after dark. A warm sleeping bag matters more than a big tent.
  • Keep Lights Low
  • Use a red head torch around camp. Eyes adjust to the dark in about twenty minutes and white light resets them.
  • Learn Five Stars
  • Find the brightest stars first and work out from there. Guides on our camp nights carry a small star chart.
(Perspective)

The Varnelo View

We plan camp nights away from roads and towns so the sky stays dark. Groups stay small, and the guide sets up camp before the light goes.

Most guests say the first hour after sunset is the best part of the trip. Sit still, let the eyes adjust, and wait.

(Final Thought)

Final Thought

A desert night asks for warm clothes and patience. Bring both and the sky does the rest.
