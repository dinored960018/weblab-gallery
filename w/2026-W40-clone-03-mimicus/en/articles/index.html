Lotus Leaf Microstructure and Self-Cleaning Paint

The lotus leaf is covered with bumps 10–20 µm tall, each coated in wax crystals. Water beads up at contact angles above 160° and rolls off, carrying dirt with it. A façade paint based on this structure reached the market in 1999.

Termite Mound Ventilation and the Eastgate Centre

Inside an African termite mound the air stays near 31 °C while the outside swings between 3 and 42 °C. The Eastgate Centre in Harare copies the mound’s chimney ventilation and runs on about 10% of the cooling energy of a comparable building.

  • The Brick-and-Mortar Structure of Abalone Nacre

    Abalone nacre stacks aragonite tablets about 0.5 µm thick with thin protein layers, like bricks and mortar. Although it is mostly brittle mineral, its fracture toughness is roughly 3,000 times that of the pure crystal.

    Last updated on 2026-07-24 by PHYLLO Rangers

  • UV Bands in Spider Webs and Bird-Safe Glass

    Some orb-weaving spiders add bands that reflect ultraviolet light to their webs. Birds see UV and steer around them. Architectural glass with a pattern visible to birds but clear to people appeared in 2006.

    Last updated on 2026-07-10 by PHYLLO Rangers

  • The Namib Desert Beetle and Fog-Harvesting Mesh

    A Namib Desert beetle collects water from morning fog on its back. Droplets form on water-loving bumps and, once heavy, roll down waxy grooves to its mouth. Fog nets built on this idea gather several litres per square metre per day.

    Last updated on 2026-06-26 by PHYLLO Rangers

  • Shock Absorption in the Woodpecker Skull

    A woodpecker strikes wood more than 20 times per second, and its head decelerates at over 1,000 g. The beak, hyoid bone and spongy bone share the load. Shock absorbers modelled on this have been studied for protecting flight recorders.

    Last updated on 2026-06-12 by PHYLLO Rangers

  • Mussel Foot Proteins and Underwater Adhesives

    Mussels anchor themselves to wave-washed rock with fine threads called byssus. The proteins at the thread tips are rich in DOPA, which sticks to wet surfaces. Adhesives based on it are being studied as an alternative to surgical sutures.

    Last updated on 2026-05-29 by PHYLLO Rangers

  • Sunflower Seed Spirals and Heliostat Layout

    Sunflower seeds follow a spiral in which each seed is turned 137.5° from the last. In 2012 researchers re-planned a Spanish solar-thermal plant’s mirror field on this pattern. Land use fell by 15.8% and mirrors shaded each other less.

    Last updated on 2026-05-15 by PHYLLO Rangers

  • The Boxfish Body and Car Aerodynamics

    The boxfish has a rigid, box-shaped body of bony plates yet swims steadily. In 2005 a carmaker presented a concept car based on its shape, with a drag coefficient of 0.19.

    Last updated on 2026-05-01 by PHYLLO Rangers

  • Bat Echolocation and the Ultrasonic Cane

    Bats emit ultrasound and read the echoes to sense their surroundings. In 2004 a team at the University of Leeds put this into a white cane that signals obstacles ahead and at head height through vibrations in the handle.

    Last updated on 2026-04-17 by PHYLLO Rangers

  • Moth-Eye Nanostructures and Anti-Reflective Film

    The eyes of night-flying moths are covered with bumps about 200 nm apart. They cut reflection so predators see the eyes less. Films based on this bring reflectance below 1%.

    Last updated on 2026-04-03 by PHYLLO Rangers

  • Spinning Maple Seeds and Tiny Flying Sensors

    A maple seed spins on its single wing and falls slowly because the rotation keeps a vortex on the leading edge. In 2021 a Northwestern University team used the principle to build 1 mm flying sensors.

    Last updated on 2026-03-20 by PHYLLO Rangers
